A frame is a box on the page that holds text, a table, or a picture. There are two ways to make one.

## Insert › Frame…

The dialog asks for **Width**, **Height**, **Down from top**, and **In from left**, in inches. The first time it offers 2.00 by 2.00 at 4.50 down and 3.25 in; after that it remembers the last frame you made, drawn ones included. A value that would put the frame off the paper is refused in place with the reason. Frames are at least a quarter inch each way.

- **Create** makes the frame on the page at the centre of the window.
- **Draw on Page** closes the dialog and lets you draw it.
- **Cancel** closes the dialog.

## Insert › Draw Frame

Choose Draw Frame (or the square button at the end of the action bar), then press on a page and drag. A dashed outline follows the pointer and the release makes the frame. A press and release in the same spot, Esc, or a press outside every page ends drawing without a frame.

## The new frame

It is an empty text frame, placed Where placed with the default frame settings, on top of the other frames of its page, and selected. One Create is one undo step. Type in it by pressing Enter or double-clicking it. To make it hold a picture instead, see [Pictures in frames](frame-pictures); for a table, Insert › Table… puts one in a frame of its own.

[Frame Properties](frame-properties) changes everything about it afterwards.
