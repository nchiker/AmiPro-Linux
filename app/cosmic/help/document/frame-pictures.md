A picture or drawing always lives in a frame; a frame holds exactly one of text, a table, or a picture. That is why there is no loose picture in the main text, and why placement, wrap, lines, and shadow for a picture are all the frame's.

## Getting a picture into a frame

- Select an empty frame and use Insert › Image…; the picture goes into it.
- With the caret in the main text, Insert › Image… makes a frame for it at the caret.
- Pasting an image or dropping a file on the page also makes a frame.

See [Inserting pictures](inserting-pictures) for the details.

## What belongs to the frame

The frame decides where the picture is, how text wraps around it, and its border, shadow, and background colour: all in [Frame Properties](frame-properties) and its pages [placement and wrap](frame-placement-wrap) and [lines and shadow](frame-lines-shadow). The picture inside is fitted to the frame by Graphics Scaling; see [Graphics scaling](graphics-scaling) and [Picture frame actions](picture-frame-actions).

Resizing a frame resizes the picture's space, and the scaling choice then decides how the picture fills it. Deleting the frame deletes the picture.
