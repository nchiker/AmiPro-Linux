A tab stop is a position that the Tab key jumps to. Tabs can be kept in three places, and the nearest one wins.

## Where tabs come from

1. **The paragraph's own ruler** (see [Rulers](rulers)): tabs placed on that paragraph alone.
2. **The paragraph's style**: set in Style Properties › Tabs, where the check box Use style tabs says whether the style has tabs of its own.
3. **The page layout**: tabs for every paragraph on pages that use the layout, set in Page Layout Properties › Tabs.

If a paragraph has its own ruler, its tabs replace those of the style and the page layout.

## Kinds and leaders

A tab is Left, Center, Right, or Decimal. A leader fills the gap before the tab with dots, hyphens, or a line, or with nothing.

## On the ruler

The button at the ruler's left end shows the kind that a click will add: click it for the next kind, or right-click it to choose the kind and leader. Click in the upper half of the ruler to add a tab there. Drag a tab to move it. Drag it down onto the page to remove it. Right-clicking a tab offers its kind, its leader, and Remove Tab; right-clicking empty ruler offers Clear Tabs.

In the style and page layout panels each tab is a row with its kind, position, and leader, plus an Add tab button.
